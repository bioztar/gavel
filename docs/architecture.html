1 / 3 — the live path

Off the live path.

what built it, and what proved it was any good

These four never run during a meeting. They are how the code got written in 13 hours and how we know the chair says something sane when she interrupts.

Devin build

Autonomous build lanes, working in parallel with us.

Ran its own branches against the repo and opened PRs like a teammate. Outcome: 3 PRs merged into main — real shipped code, not a demo.

Quality Clouds — Norma code quality

AI code-quality analysis over the repository.

Scanned gavel and came back with concrete findings. Outcome: findings fixed in the codebase before freeze — the scan changed the code, it was not a logo on a slide.

Galtea evals

Evaluating the one model-shaped output: the sentence she says.

Ten frozen cases — floor hog at 62% and 81%, topic over budget, a silent must-hear attendee, 15 seconds of dead air. Traces sent up and scored on whether she names the right person, stays under 20 words, hands the floor somewhere real, stays polite, and invents nothing she never heard. Outcome: scored runs, not guesses.

Langfuse tracing

Observability on every model call, via Mastra's exporter.

One trace session per meeting, so a bad interruption can be opened and read back to the exact prompt and state that produced it. Outcome: the chair is debuggable — wired in brain, on when the keys are set.

The split that matters: whether to interrupt is plain code, unit-tested against a scripted replay. Only what she says goes to a model — so only that needs evals.

Where this goes.

Discord was the harness, not the product

We built on Discord because it let us get a bot into a live voice call in an afternoon. Nothing about the chair depends on it.

The chair is platform-agnostic by construction: one service owns the call connection, everything else talks HTTP. Swapping Discord for Meet, Zoom or Teams means writing one new adapter against the same wire — the agenda, the floor clock, the triggers, the voice and the avatar are untouched. That seam is already load-bearing today: ears-discord is the only container that knows what a voice call even is.
  • The agenda already exists. It is the calendar invite the organiser sent — we read it, nobody retypes it.
  • She acts inside the hour. A chair who interrupts at minute 12 is worth more than a summary at minute 61.
  • It leaves a record with numbers. Floor time per person, per topic, per meeting — the first honest data most orgs have on where the hour went.
Google Meet
Workspace add-on. Lives beside Calendar, where the agenda already comes from.
Zoom
Zoom App + bot SDK. The meeting-heavy install base and the clearest pain.
Microsoft Teams
The enterprise suite play: sold with M365, not alongside it.

Not another meeting-notes bot. The category is time governance: who gets heard, what gets decided, whether the hour was worth its payroll cost — live, while it can still be changed, not in a summary afterwards.
