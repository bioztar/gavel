gavel — architecture reset & platform plan

Strip the hackathon scaffolding. Keep the chair. Google Meet first, then Zoom, then Teams.

Working document for the Vitaly × Artem review · 21 September 2026 · bioztar/gavel

The chair's core costs nothing to run. Everything expensive in the repo today is optional, and most of it exists because of a 36-hour hackathon, not because of the product.

gavel decides whether to interrupt from two events and a clock — speaking.start, speaking.end. No audio decoding, no model call, no vendor. Only what she says hits a model: one sentence, under 20 words.

That means the judged moment — "Vitaly, you've had eight of the last ten minutes — Ana, you had a point on this" — runs for approximately zero marginal cost. The expensive parts (avatar video, continuous STT, restreaming) are all removable without touching it.

Why this is the whole strategy in one line

Adding Google Meet is one new package and nothing else. The agenda format, the floor clock, the triggers, the voice, the notes, the evals and the console all stay as they are. Any estimate that says otherwise is estimating a rewrite that is not needed.

The avatar is the single largest cost and the most fragile machinery in the repo

It exists for one reason: Discord blocks bot video outright, so the chair needed a face on a separate projector page. That constraint does not exist on Meet, Zoom or Teams — a bot participant has its own tile and a static image fills it for $0.

What comes out with it: a browser that owns the RTCPeerConnection because Python cannot, an SSE command channel, a heartbeat that by the author's own note "cannot billing-stop anything", a token-gated fal proxy, and a committed esbuild bundle. That is 7,487 lines of the most failure-prone code in the system, serving a feature no buyer asked for.

Recommendation: cut Director from the live path for v1 on every platform. Keep the persona artwork. Revisit only if a customer pays for a face.

Keep

The product. Zero platform coupling. Untouched by all of this.

ears-discord · 5,696 lines

Stays. It is the only surface that works end to end today, and therefore the regression harness that proves ears-meet is correct. Costs nothing to keep.

calendar · 4,022 lines

Promote, don't just keep. On Meet this gets more valuable — Calendar is where the agenda natively lives. Already emits the frozen contract shape, and already refuses to send an invite with no topics.

Decide

fal / chair-video · 7,487 lines

Cut from the live path — see above. This is a decision for the call, not a unilateral deletion, because it is the visual identity of the demo.

Mastra

Removable behind an interface that already exists — chair/llm.ts and chair/mastraLlm.ts are two implementations of one seam. Cost of removal: the Langfuse exporter rides Mastra's tracing (@mastra/langfuse in mastra/index.ts), so losing one loses the other. Not urgent. Not free either.

SLNG

Keep the capability, name the adapter. STT lives in ears, TTS in brain, both already adapter-shaped and swappable per deployment. Note the cross-project coupling: the Karen chat site reuses this same TTS.

Delete

stream-vonage · 777 lines

HLS restream + archive of the stage, published from a browser screen-capture. Built to satisfy a sponsor and to produce the submission video. A time-governance product has no restream requirement at all.

ears-vonage · 0 lines

A README describing a package that was never written. Credentials never arrived; it was blocked from the start.

Also going: the compose service, scripts/set-vonage-key.sh, the VONAGE_* settings, and ~15 documents that still name it.

In flight. A Devin lane is preparing this as a branch and a PR right now — chore/excise-vonage. It will not be merged before the review.

Off the live path — no runtime cost, no action needed

Devin (autonomous build lanes, 3 PRs merged), Quality Clouds (code scan, findings fixed before freeze), Galtea (ten frozen eval cases on the one model-shaped output), Langfuse (one trace session per meeting). None of these run during a meeting. They are evidence artifacts and development tooling — leave them exactly where they are.

4 · Target architecture after the reset

1 · Before

calendar

Host dictates a brief → model writes purpose, topics, owners, minutes, must-hear → refuses if there are no topics → the agenda lands in the real invite and in agenda.json.

2 · Hear

ears-<platform>

Joins the call. Emits who is speaking and when. Transcribes. Plays her audio back into the room. One package per platform, nothing else changes.

3 · Think

brain

Floor clock and triggers in plain code. One model call writes the sentence. Notes, decisions, open items, parking lot. Postgres + Redis behind it.

4 · Show

console + record

Live transcript, agenda progress, her exact words, and the number that sells it: floor time per person, per topic, per meeting.

Container count drops from eight to five — Postgres, Redis, ears-*, brain, calendar — plus one headless browser per concurrent meeting. Removed: stream-vonage, chair-video.

The unit cost moves, and it is worth naming before Artem asks

With the avatar gone, inference is not the scale driver — the browser is. Every concurrent meeting on Meet needs its own headless Chromium: roughly 1 vCPU and 0.5–1 GB RAM, for the duration of the call. That is the number that decides whether gavel is priced per room, per seat, or per meeting-hour, and it is the number a managed bot vendor is quoting you when they charge per bot-hour.

Why the Google Meet Media API is not the route — despite being the "official" one

It is the obvious first answer and it fails on three independent counts, any one of which is fatal:

  • It cannot speak. The client's SDP offer must contain receive-only media descriptions — a=recvonly from the client, a=sendonly from Meet. gavel's entire product is a chair who interrupts out loud. A receive-only API cannot be the surface.
  • It cannot count the floor. The cap is exactly three virtual audio streams, allocated to the most relevant speakers and reassigned as the conversation moves. Talk-time share per person over a whole meeting is structurally unobtainable from three rotating slots. This one is architectural, not a preview limitation — it does not go away at GA.
  • It is not generally available. Developer Preview enrolment is required for the Cloud project, the OAuth principal, and every participant in the conference. Also blocked for encrypted or watermarked meetings, blocked when an underage account is present, capped at eight hours, and the host can terminate it mid-call.

A headless browser bot is visible in the participant list — which is the honest behaviour for an AI chair anyway — and works across every platform with the same playbook.

6 · /compose becomes a browser extension

Today /compose is a page on our server: the host types a brief into one box, a model turns it into a purpose, topics, owners, minutes and a must-hear list, and it refuses to send an invite that has no topics. That gate is the reason the in-call triggers have anything to enforce — and it is currently a page nobody visits, in a product whose users already live in a calendar tab.

What the extension does

  • Adds a "Chair this meeting" control directly into Google Calendar's event editor and Meet's pre-join screen — where the meeting is actually being created.
  • Reads the event the user is already editing: title, attendees, duration, existing description.
  • Sends the brief to our server, which does the model call and returns the structured agenda.
  • Writes the agenda into the real invite description, and registers the contract-shaped agenda.json against the meeting id.
  • Shows the refusal inline when the brief has no decidable topics — the gate moves to the point of creation instead of a separate page.
  • Invites the bot account to the event, which is how gavel gets into the call.

How sign-in works

  • Manifest V3, service worker background, content scripts scoped to the calendar and meeting hosts only.
  • Google: chrome.identity.getAuthToken on Chrome, launchWebAuthFlow for cross-browser and for Microsoft and Zoom. The user signs in with the account they already use.
  • Scopes stay minimal and are named in the consent screen: read and write the events the user is editing. Nothing org-wide.
  • The extension holds no API key. It holds a user OAuth token and nothing else — every model call, every provider credential and every write to our database stays server-side. This is non-negotiable: an extension is client code and anything shipped in it is public.
  • Tokens live in chrome.storage.session, never in localStorage, and are exchanged server-side for our own session.

Distribution is two doors, and they are not the same door

Chrome Web Store + Edge Add-ons is the self-serve route — an individual installs it, no admin involved, which is how you get the first hundred users. Google Workspace Marketplace is the enterprise route — an admin deploys it org-wide, which is how you get the first paying contract. Build for the first, design the permissions so the second is possible without a rewrite.

8 · What needs deciding — with Artem, on the call

1 · Build ears-meet, or buy bot infrastructure?

Managed meeting-bot vendors (Recall.ai, MeetStream, Meeting BaaS) run the Chromium fleet, handle joining, speaker attribution and transcripts across all three platforms behind one integration — priced per bot-hour.

BuyDays instead of weeks, all three platforms at once, someone else owns the breakage when Meet changes its DOM. Costs per meeting-hour forever, and the speaking-event granularity we need for Tier 0 has to be verified against their API before committing.
BuildOwns the margin, owns the data, no per-hour floor under the pricing. Weeks of work per platform and we own every UI change Google ships.

Worth stating plainly: "no sponsor-specific solutions" means dropping vendors chosen for hackathon prize eligibility. It does not mean refusing all third parties. A bot vendor is a legitimate option and may be the efficient answer for getting to a first customer.

2 · Does Karen keep a face?

$216 per 45-minute call, ~7,500 lines, the most fragile subsystem in the repo — against a demo moment that genuinely lands in a room.

Cut for v1Recommended. Static persona image in the bot's tile. Reversible: the package stays in git history and the audio path is unchanged.
Keep for sales demos onlyBehind a flag, off by default, never on a customer meeting. Costs the maintenance but keeps the wow for a pitch.

3 · Whose account does the bot use?

The bot must be a participant, which means it must be an identity somewhere.

Bring your ownThe customer creates a bot account in their own Workspace. Solves external-participant blocks and data residency instantly; adds an onboarding step that loses self-serve users.
Managed by usOne click to install, and then a fight with every Workspace that blocks external participants.

This is a go-to-market decision wearing a technical costume — it decides whether gavel is self-serve or sales-led.

4 · Mastra: keep or unwind?

Not urgent, but it should be a decision rather than a drift. The swap seam already exists in the code.

KeepLangfuse tracing comes free through its exporter, and "the chair is debuggable" is a real claim we make. Cost is one more framework in the dependency tree.
Unwindchair/llm.ts already defines the interface; direct calls are a small change. Then Langfuse needs wiring directly, or we lose per-meeting traces.

One open technical question this document cannot answer from documentation

Can a Zoom Meeting SDK bot on Linux send raw audio into the meeting? Capture is thoroughly documented; injection is not. Zoom's Video SDK has full raw media control but is a different product with a different compliance profile. This is a half-day spike and it should happen before any Zoom date is given to anyone. Google Meet and Teams egress are both settled — virtual mic and native sendrecv respectively.
